# What to do, and who to follow

## This site is not a warning service

This site shows public weather data, place by place, and archives past events. We do not issue warnings, and nothing here replaces them. For warnings and forecasts follow **IPMA**, the Portuguese Institute for Sea and Atmosphere: https://www.ipma.pt/. For civil protection notices follow **ANEPC**, the national civil protection authority: https://prociv.gov.pt/. Also follow your **municipality's civil protection** service (*Serviço Municipal de Proteção Civil*); we have not listed individual municipalities, so look for yours on your council's official site.

## IPMA's warning levels

IPMA colours each warning area (a district, or a Madeira or Azores region) for each type of weather: green, yellow, orange or red. From IPMA's users guide (https://www.ipma.pt/en/enciclopedia/otempo/sam/index.html, read 17 Nov 2026):

- **Green:** no hazardous weather conditions foreseen.
- **Yellow:** weather-sensitive activities may be affected; pay attention to how the weather develops.
- **Orange:** weather conditions involving moderate to high risk; keep informed and follow the advice of the civil protection authorities.
- **Red:** weather conditions involving high risk; follow civil protection guidance.

The wording above is our summary of IPMA's page; check the page itself. IPMA also publishes the thresholds behind each level, set per type of weather and per area (https://www.ipma.pt/en/enciclopedia/otempo/sam/index.html?page=criterios.xml, read 17 Nov 2026). For mainland Portugal the page gives, for example, maximum wind gusts of 70 to 90 km/h for yellow, 91 to 130 km/h for orange and above 130 km/h for red; and for rough sea (significant wave height) 4 to 5 m yellow, 5 to 7 m orange, above 7 m red. A warning's own text can give different figures for its district; read the warning itself.

IPMA's current warnings, by district, are on https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/ (read 17 Nov 2026). That page lists a colour for each district and the time of its last update, and states that times are UTC. It does not itself define the colours; the definitions are on the users guide above. Note that IPMA's open data can lag its warning messages by an hour or more, as the Storm Charles record found (see our archive record), so read IPMA's own page and its update time.

## IPMA's rural fire danger classes

IPMA publishes a rural fire danger map (*Risco de Incêndio Rural*, RCM) for mainland Portugal (https://www.ipma.pt/en/riscoincendio/rcm.pt/ and the Portuguese version https://www.ipma.pt/pt/riscoincendio/rcm.pt/, read 17 Nov 2026). The classes are, in order: *reduzido* (low), *moderado* (moderate), *elevado* (high), *muito elevado* (very high) and *máximo* (maximum).

**Source gap:** the IPMA pages we read name only *muito elevado* and *máximo*, in the text on restrictions (in municipalities at those two levels, agricultural and forestry machinery may be used from sunset to 11:00 despite the usual restrictions; a government-declared alert situation overrides the fire danger class conditions). They do not list or define all five classes. The five names above are the classes used in IPMA's fire risk data, which this site reads; they are not confirmed on a page we read, and IPMA's own definitions of each class are not yet recorded here.

## What to do

These lists use only what we could read on official pages. Where a page gave no advice, we say so rather than write our own.

**In a storm (strong wind, heavy rain).** Gap: ANEPC's Aviso 21 on Storm Charles (https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/avisos-a-populacao/aviso-21-depressao-charles-agravamento-da-situacao-meteorologica-chuva-e-vento-forte-medidas-preventivas/, read 17 Nov 2026) returned only its title, status and date (09.11.2026) to our reader, not its list of preventive measures; the ANEPC news item on its SMS warning (https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/noticias/depressao-charles-anepc-avisa-populacao-por-sms/, dated 10.11.2026, read 17 Nov 2026) gave no advice; and ANEPC's natural risks page (https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/prevencao-e-preparacao/avaliacao-de-riscos/riscos-naturais/, read 17 Nov 2026) describes strong wind but lists no self-protection measures, and for floods only says that each citizen in a flood-risk area should know the self-protection measures, without listing them. What IPMA says (users guide, read 17 Nov 2026): at orange, keep informed and follow the civil protection authorities' advice. Read the full ANEPC notice for the event on https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/avisos-a-populacao/.

**In rough seas.** Gap: no ANEPC or IPMA self-protection advice for rough sea was found on the pages read. ANEPC's notices list (https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/avisos-a-populacao/, read 17 Nov 2026) showed no sea-swell notice at that time. IPMA's rough-sea warning levels are in the thresholds above (4 to 5 m, 5 to 7 m, above 7 m for mainland Portugal).

**In heat.** From ANEPC's natural risks page, section on heat waves, "Medidas de Autoproteção" (https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/prevencao-e-preparacao/avaliacao-de-riscos/riscos-naturais/, read 17 Nov 2026), in our English:

- look for cool, ventilated places;
- drink water or natural juices regularly, even if you are not thirsty;
- avoid hot, alcoholic, fizzy, caffeinated and sugary drinks;
- avoid direct sun in the hottest hours, in particular 11:00 to 17:00;
- use sunscreen of factor 30 or higher, every two hours;
- wear light, loose, light-coloured clothes, preferably cotton, with a hat and sunglasses;
- avoid activities that need great physical effort, such as sport or outdoor leisure;
- do not stay in parked vehicles exposed to the sun;
- eat light meals, and more often;
- watch for heat warnings, and take special care of people most vulnerable to heat.

The page also points to the national health service guide on heat (https://www.sns24.gov.pt/guia/proteja-se-contra-o-calor/), which we did not read.

**In high rural fire danger.** Gap: no ANEPC self-protection list for rural fires was found on the pages read; ANEPC's natural risks page does not treat rural fires. What we can source: ANEPC publishes rural fire danger notices (for example Aviso 12, rural fire danger, dated 01.07.2026) on https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/avisos-a-populacao/ (read 17 Nov 2026), and IPMA's fire danger page (above) shows restrictions that depend on the class and on any declared alert situation. Follow the restrictions in force for your municipality.

## Who to follow

- **IPMA** (warnings, forecasts, fire danger): https://www.ipma.pt/ . Warnings by district: https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/ .
- **ANEPC** (national civil protection: notices to the public, alerts): https://prociv.gov.pt/ ; notices at https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/avisos-a-populacao/ . On 10 November 2026 its news item said it warned the population by free SMS ahead of Storm Charles, with no sign-up needed (page read 17 Nov 2026).
- **Your municipality's civil protection:** through your council's official site.
- **112, the emergency number.** ANEPC's home page (https://prociv.gov.pt/, read 17 Nov 2026) says: in an emergency, call 112. Gap: the page we tried on the European Union's Your Europe site (https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/security-and-emergencies/emergency-assistance/index_en.htm, read 17 Nov 2026) did not describe 112 in the text we read, so we have no page read that states it is the European emergency number.

We list only official pages. We give no phone numbers other than 112.
